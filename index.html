AI Preparedness in Computer Science Education

A UWP research blog on AI preparedness in undergraduate computer science education.

Read the full argument Preview the thesis
Main argument

CS programs still matter, but they are not fully caught up with AI-shaped software work.

Generative AI is changing how software is written, debugged, reviewed, and explored. That does not make computer science education irrelevant. It exposes which parts of CS education still matter most, especially foundations, reasoning, and judgment, while also revealing what programs are not yet teaching clearly enough.

Based on current research and a personal interview with Justin Jia, a Senior Software Engineer at Apple, my argument is that CS programs are still valuable because they establish foundational knowledge students should not dismiss. However, they are not fully caught up with the industry expectation that students know how to integrate AI into engineering workflows, judge AI-generated code, and navigate the messy reality of large codebases that define professional software engineering.

Core claim

Universities should not abandon fundamentals or turn every CS class into an AI tools class. They should make AI literacy and long-term software judgment more visible across the curriculum.

Research focus

Questions guiding the blog

  1. How should CS students define preparedness when AI is part of software work?
  2. Which foundations remain essential for judging AI-generated code?
  3. How can students learn to work inside large codebases, not only short assignments?
  4. What should universities teach so AI becomes a tool instead of a replacement brain?

Evidence base

Sources used in the argument

Student anxiety

Farooqi et al. (2026) frames how AI is already shaping computer science students' career fears and uncertainty around entry-level roles.

Developer workflow

Shao and Ishengoma (2026) and Brandebusemeyer et al. (2025) support the claim that AI helps most when developers review and integrate its output carefully.

AI literacy and curriculum

Cerny (2024) and Smith (2024) help define AI literacy and explain why CS educators are still negotiating AI's place in the curriculum.
